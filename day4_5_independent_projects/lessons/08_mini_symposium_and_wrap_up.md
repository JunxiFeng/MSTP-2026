# Mini-Symposium and Wrap-Up

**ADVANCED**

## Format

Nine of you, nine independent projects — each presents for about 4 minutes, followed by 1-2 minutes of questions. Order doesn't matter; go in whatever order gets everyone through in the 45-minute slot. You present your own work, alone — even if you shared a dataset with someone else in the room, this is your analysis and your conclusions.

## What a 4-minute talk needs, and doesn't need

State your biological question and why it matters, your independent unit and comparison in one sentence each, your headline figure, and your conclusion scoped to what you actually analyzed (checklist item 28). You do not need a literature review, a methods deep-dive, or every figure you made — pick the one or two that carry the argument. A clean null result is a complete, presentable talk on its own; Day 3's own compositional analysis found nothing significant and that was still worth a full section.

## The rubric

No numeric score — PASS/WARNING/FAIL per dimension, same ethos as every checklist this course has used:

| Dimension | PASS | WARNING | FAIL |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Question & scope | Stated and locked in before results, matches checklist item 25 | Scope stated but visibly adjusted after seeing a result | No stated scope, or scope invented after the fact |
| Independent-unit handling | Donor/sample/animal-level unit used correctly throughout | Correct in most but not all steps | Cells or lanes treated as independent replicates |
| Statistical rigor | Pseudobulk, correction, and pairing (where applicable) all handled correctly for the design | One of the three present but imperfect | None of the three addressed |
| Pre-computed annotation trust (if applicable) | Any source annotation spot-checked independently | Used without checking, but flagged as a caveat | Used and presented as ground truth, unflagged |
| Visualization | Follows the course's dataviz defaults; legend/labels correct; no dual-axis or rainbow | Readable but a palette/legend/label lapse | Misleading or unreadable |
| Interpretation scope | Claim matches the actual species/tissue/timepoint/n | Mostly scoped, one overreach | Generalizes well beyond the data |

This is the same instrument your reviewer already ran on you in [07_peer_review.md](07_peer_review.md) — the mini-symposium audience (instructors, TAs, everyone else) is applying it fresh, which is why a reviewer's earlier WARNING is worth surfacing during Q&A rather than treating peer review as a one-and-done step.

## Whole-bootcamp wrap-up

Five days ago, "the terminal" and "git" were the hard parts. Today you took a real public dataset nobody had walked you through, decided your own scope, ran a pipeline you're now practiced enough in to have opinions about, defended it against a peer, and presented it — on your own. That arc — from needing every step explained to being the one explaining your choices to someone else — is what this week was actually for. The tools you used will change; the habit of stating your independent unit, your comparison, and your scope before you look at a result won't, and it's the one thing worth carrying out of this room.
